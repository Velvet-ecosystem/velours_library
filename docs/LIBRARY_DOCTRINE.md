# Velour's Library Doctrine

Velour's Library exists to make durable knowledge available to Velvet without confusing possession of information with understanding or truth.

The library should be able to answer four questions about every useful item:

1. What is it?
2. Where did it come from?
3. What happened to it after acquisition?
4. Why should this result be trusted, doubted, or treated as uncertain?

## Doctrine

- The library preserves evidence before optimizing convenience.
- Originals and derivatives remain distinguishable.
- Search quality and source authority are separate dimensions.
- Offline capability is a first-class requirement, not a degraded fallback.
- Indexes are rebuildable; provenance is not disposable.
- Human-authored, machine-generated, and extracted content remain explicitly distinguishable.
- Knowledge packs may be moved between Velvet bodies without requiring those bodies to share identical models or compute.
- A missing Internet connection must not erase already acquired knowledge.
- A source may be useful even when it is wrong, outdated, or disputed, provided its state is represented truthfully.
- The library should surface disagreement rather than silently manufacture consensus.

Velour is responsible for keeping the shelves honest. Velvet remains responsible for reasoning with what comes off them.
